# New-topic syllabus (ordered for a Russian-speaking developer)

Work through in order; each entry: teaching angle + typical Russian-speaker traps.

1. **present-perfect-vs-past** (Present perfect vs past simple) - Russian has one past tense plus aspect, so the have-done/did split does not map. Teach: result-now vs finished-time. Traps: "I already fixed it yesterday" mixing, "did you ever use" for experience.
2. **question-formation** (Questions and auxiliaries) - Russian asks with intonation and word order; English requires do/does/did or inversion. Traps: "why it fails?", "does it returns?", embedded questions ("I wonder does it work").
3. **conditionals** (Real and unreal conditionals) - Russian uses "если + будет" freely; English bans "will" in the if-clause. Traps: "if it will fail", "if I would know".
4. **gerund-vs-infinitive** (Gerund vs infinitive) - stop doing / stop to do change meaning; verbs select one form arbitrarily. Traps: "suggest to do", "avoid to use", "worth to try".
5. **countable-uncountable** (Uncountables beyond articles) - advice, research, feedback, information, progress, money take no plural and singular verbs. Traps: "advices", "researches", "informations", "a progress".
6. **adverb-placement** (Adverb position) - frequency adverbs sit before the main verb but after "be". Traps: "always I check", "I check always the logs" (verb-object must not be split).
7. **future-forms** (will vs going to vs present continuous) - Russian has one future; English picks by plan vs prediction vs arrangement. Traps: will for fixed plans ("I will meet him tomorrow at 3" when it is scheduled).
8. **phrasal-verbs-core** (Core phrasal verbs) - set up, figure out, roll out, point out, work around, fall back; particle placement with pronouns ("set it up", never "set up it").
9. **relative-clauses** (Relative clauses and commas) - that vs which/who, and the comma rule Russian reverses: English forbids a comma before restrictive "that/which", Russian requires one before "который". Traps: "the test, that fails".
10. **comma-splices** (Joining clauses) - Russian happily joins clauses with a comma; English needs a conjunction, semicolon, or period. Traps: "it compiles, it still fails at runtime".
11. **modals-nuance** (must / have to / should / might) - obligation source, advice strength, and probability grading. Traps: "must" where "have to" is natural, "can" for possibility where "might" fits.
12. **reported-speech** (Reported speech and backshift) - tense backshift and pronoun shifts absent in Russian. Traps: "he said it works" vs "he said it worked", "asked that I do".
13. **passive-usage** (Passive voice, natural use) - when passive reads better in English (processes, bug reports) and get-passives. Traps: overusing reflexive-style actives calqued from Russian ("the file deleted itself").
14. **used-to-would** (Past habits) - used to / would for past habits, "be used to doing" vs "used to do". Traps: "I was used to write" for a past habit.
15. **articles-advanced** (Zero article and generics) - bare generics (users, data), institutions (in production, at work, on call), fixed phrases. Traps: "the production", "a staff".
16. **inversion-emphasis** (Emphatic structures) - not only ... but also, neither do I, so does it; cleft sentences ("it is the cache that breaks"). Traps: "not only it is slow".
